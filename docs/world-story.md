# World and story bible

## The world
One interconnected, bounded sea with twelve **fixed** islands. Three large, five medium and four small islands. Each has a consistent silhouette, landing place, at least one memorable activity and people or wildlife. Sea travel should be short enough to stay interesting, with birds, distant sails, waves, boats and fishing spots between destinations. Use atmospheric mist occasionally, but do not build the plot around a lost-at-sea mystery.

## The cast
- **Protagonist (working name Rowan):** fascinated with boats since childhood, worked and saved for years, finally purchased a small fishing/sailing boat. Curious, kind and practical.
- **Woman he hopes to woo (working name Mira):** an adult with her own job, friends and love of coastal sketching and visiting new places. Her choices matter; a date is an invitation, not an item purchase.
- **Harbourmaster:** introduces the town and offers the first delivery.
- **Fish seller:** teaches fish values and accepts catches.
- **Saltglass fisher:** introduces the island's fishing spots and welcomes travellers.
- Later: boatwright, carpenter, market traders, farmers, lighthouse keeper, musicians and festival-goers.
Names, visual designs and exact writing style are provisional until the creator approves them.

## Story in acts
**Act I — First Voyage:** optional childhood memory, adult introduction after buying the boat, meet neighbours, see Mira, take a short paid job, fish, sail to Saltglass Bay, walk ashore, return and begin saving for a seaside plot.

**Act II — Finding a life:** grow reputation through paid island work; visit the rest of the archipelago; explore three candidate plots (one town-adjacent, one fishing-village-adjacent, one secluded); take optional trips with Mira and deepen their connection through authored scenes.

**Act III — A home of our own:** agree on a future together, purchase one plot, fund an actual construction sequence via fair work and local suppliers, furnish and enjoy a small house by the sea. The sea and islands remain accessible after the ending.

## Narrative craft
Favor short warm lines, specific moments and environmental storytelling over heavy exposition. Recurring NPCs should remember what has happened. Track quests and relationship beats as stable data IDs; save changes atomically. Avoid anonymous affection points, manipulative romance mechanics, obligatory marriage, expensive gift grinding or generic AI-generated dialogue.

Every new island must justify itself with a destination, an activity, a character encounter and a distinctive visual moment. Every story scene should either help the player understand a person or progress toward the dream. Allow activities to be repeated without blocking story pacing.
